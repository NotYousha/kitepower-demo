Why altitude

The wind a tower can’t reach

Wind speed rises with height, and power rises with the cube of wind speed. A kite reaches the stronger air for a fraction of the steel, because the tether carries the load instead of a tower.

Illustrative: textbook one-seventh power law, neutral atmosphere, relative to an 80 m turbine hub. Phase 1 LiDAR measures the real profile at the test site.

+21%

Faster wind at 300 m than at a typical 80 m turbine hub.

+76%

More power in that wind, because power scales with the cube of speed.

−90%

Less material than a tower turbine of the same output: a fabric wing, a tether and a ground winch.

48 h

From shipping container to first power, with no crane or foundation. That matters where the only access is a summer ferry or an airstrip.

How it works

The pumping cycle

The wing flies fast figure-eights across the wind, pulling tether off a drum that spins a generator. Then it depowers, glides back, and a motor reels it in using a fraction of the energy.

One cycle, to scale. (80 s × 65 kW − 20 s × 10 kW) ÷ 100 s = 50 kW net.

  1. Reel-out · 80 seconds

    Crosswind flight at high tension drives the generator at 65 kW while 160 m of tether pays out.

  2. Reel-in · 20 seconds

    The wing pitches to near-zero pull and the winch recovers the tether, drawing 10 kW.

  3. Repeat, with a battery smoothing the output

    A small buffer battery turns the pulse into steady power for the grid. Proven at 20 to 200 kW by TU Delft and European kite-power firms. Loyd (1980) gives the physics: power scales with wing area, the cube of wind speed and the square of lift-to-drag.

The product

K-50, and the software that flies it

One 20-foot container holds the wing, ground station and controls. Three choices are specific to our climate: a heated drum and ice-shedding wing coating, an automated mast launch that needs no crew, and a microgrid controller that leaves the utility operator in charge.

Market

Start where power costs a dollar. Then go everywhere diesel is.

Utility-scale airborne wind has to beat cheap grid power, and that is where earlier efforts struggled. Remote diesel grids already pay three times our cost. That is the entry point.

20 systemsNewfoundland and Labrador

50 GWh a year, about 326 K-50 equivalents. Target 24 units by Year 5, seven percent of isolated diesel generation. First sites: Ramea, Black Tickle, Makkovik.

More than 740 million litres of diesel a year across Nunavut, the northern territories and remote First Nations. The federal Wah-ila-toos hub committed $300 million to diesel reduction through 2027.
